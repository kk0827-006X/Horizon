Community contributors expand the list with additional tells like contrastive negation and LinkedIn clichés. As LLM-generated content proliferates, being able to detect it is crucial for maintaining authenticity in writing. The discussion also highlights how LLMs can be used to improve human writing without sacrificing personal style. The post and comments identify dozens of specific phrases and structures, including 'It's not X, it's Y' patterns and repetitive n-gram usage. Some commenters note that LLM output often feels superior to human writing in areas where the reader lacks expertise, making detection challenging.

**Background**: 'Code smells' is a software engineering term for surface-level indicators of deeper problems in code. Similarly, 'LLM smells' are linguistic patterns that betray AI generation. Research shows LLM-generated text often has a higher incidence of certain patterns compared to human writing, and the concept is increasingly important as LLMs are used for writing assistance.

## [Continue? Y/N: A 60-second game simulating AI agent permission fatigue](https://llmgame.scalex.dev/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A 60-second web game titled 'Continue? Y/N' has been published, simulating the experience of reviewing AI agent permission requests and highlighting the issue of permission fatigue. This game brings attention to the critical problem of AI agent permission fatigue, where users tend to approve requests without careful review, potentially compromising security. It prompts discussions on how to balance usability and safety in AI agent design. The game's scoring system allows players to achieve a high score by denying all requests rapidly, which can bypass the intended evaluation of security diligence. Additionally, some commands flagged as unsafe, such as 'cat ~/.zshrc', are debated because many users do not store secrets in their shell configuration files.

hackernews · Wirbelwind · May 28, 13:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48308376)

**Background**: AI agents are software programs that can perform tasks autonomously, often requiring permissions to execute commands on a user's system. Permission fatigue occurs when users become desensitized to frequent permission prompts, leading to careless approvals. This game simulates that scenario to raise awareness.
