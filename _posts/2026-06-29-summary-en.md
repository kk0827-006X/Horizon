## [Memory prices from 1960 to 2026: long-term decline, recent volatility](https://dam.stanford.edu/memory-prices.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A visualization and dataset from Stanford tracks memory prices per gigabyte from 1960 to 2026, showing a dramatic long-term decline but recent volatility due to crypto and AI demand. This dataset provides a valuable historical perspective on computing costs, illustrating how Moore's Law drove exponential price drops and how new market forces have disrupted the trend. The prices are not inflation-adjusted, so early costs appear even higher in real terms, and per-GB pricing before 1990 is unrealistic since no one purchased gigabytes then.

hackernews · vga1 · Jun 28, 18:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48710092)

**Background**: Memory prices have declined dramatically since the 1960s, driven by Moore's Law and manufacturing scale. However, recent demand from cryptocurrency mining and AI has caused price spikes, flattening the curve in the 2010s as Moore's Law slowed.

**Discussion**: Comments note that inflation adjustment would steepen the early curve, and that comparing modern DDR5 to 2010 DDR3 pricing suggests a rollback to that era. Users also blame crypto and AI for recent volatility.

## [Tokenmaxxing is dead, long live tokenmaxxing](https://12gramsofcarbon.com/p/agentics-tech-things-tokenmaxxing) ⭐️ 6.0/10

An opinion piece argues that the old approach of minimizing token usage (tokenmaxxing) is outdated, and that spending more tokens now leads to better results through a phenomenon called 'compounding correctness'. This challenges a common productivity mindset in AI workflows, potentially shifting how companies measure and optimize AI usage, and could affect the design of agentic systems that rely on iterative token expenditure. The article introduces 'compounding correctness' as the idea that more tokens generally yield better outcomes, in contrast to the prior regime where minimizing tokens was critical. The author presented these views at an Agentics meetup.

hackernews · theahura · Jun 28, 16:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48708795)

**Background**: Tokenmaxxing is a Silicon Valley buzzword derived from Gen Z slang, meaning to maximize AI token usage often beyond actual task requirements, sometimes as a productivity metric. The discussion around LLM error compounding shows that each step in a multi-step LLM system carries a risk of error, accumulating over steps. The author claims a new regime has emerged where spending more tokens reduces error compounding and improves correctness.
