## [Weave Router: Smart AI model routing for coding agents](https://github.com/workweave/router) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Weave Router is an open-source tool that integrates with coding agents like Claude Code and Cursor, using a reinforcement learning model to route each inference request to the most cost-effective model, achieving 40% token cost savings without noticeable quality loss. As AI-assisted coding becomes increasingly expensive, this router helps developers and companies significantly reduce API costs while maintaining output quality, addressing a critical pain point in the AI development ecosystem. The router is trained on tens of thousands of agent traces using reinforcement learning, and it handles model API translations automatically. It is available under Elastic License 2.0 for self-hosting, with a hosted version at weaverouter.com.

## [German Court Holds Google Liable for AI Overview Errors](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/25/ai-and-liability/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A German court ruled that Google is legally liable for errors in its AI-generated overviews, establishing that AI agents are treated as agents of the deploying company. This landmark ruling sets a precedent that companies cannot use AI as a shield to avoid liability, potentially reshaping corporate responsibility and AI deployment practices globally. The ruling draws an analogy to human agency: if a company hires human writers, it is liable for their errors; the same legal principle applies to AI systems deployed by the company.

## [New ultrasound brain imaging stirs excitement and concern](https://alephneuro.com/blog/ultrasound-brain) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A novel ultrasound technique for brain imaging achieves high resolution by injecting sparse microbubbles, but it has not been validated against established methods like MRI. If validated, this could enable portable, low-cost brain imaging, potentially expanding access in remote or resource-limited settings. However, safety concerns and the lack of direct comparison with existing modalities like MRI temper the excitement. The technique relies on injecting sparse sulfur hexafluoride microbubbles as contrast agents; the super-resolution method depends critically on bubble sparseness. Transitioning to a bubble-free imaging approach, as the authors aspire, remains a significant challenge.

hackernews · rossant · Jun 26, 11:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48685558)

**Background**: Functional ultrasound imaging (fUS) is an emerging neuroimaging modality that measures hemodynamic changes, offering high spatiotemporal resolution. However, transcranial imaging is limited by skull-induced aberrations. The new technique aims to overcome this, but safety concerns persist, as even low-dose ultrasound can cause ultrastructural changes in brain tissue.
